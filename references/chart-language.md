# The chart language

The visual grammar for multi-universe story charts. Written for THE WAIF,
general. These rules encode an ontology; violating them changes the story the
chart tells.

## Lanes

- One vertical lane per universe the protagonist occupies. Universes are the
  units; time flows DOWN.
- Order lanes so the thread migrates one lane per crossing — opening universe
  RIGHTMOST, each join one lane to the left. No long horizontals across the
  top of the chart.
- Lane label = the world's name in the film's own language (a place, an object,
  a state). Label the TIMELINE SEGMENTS too when a lane means "the world with
  her in it" rather than a place.

## Splits

- A split = an act that forks the world (a trigger pull, a jump, a choice under
  mortality). Drawn as a yellow circle carrying the SCENE number of the act.
- Two tines: the one we follow (vertical continues) and the one where the
  protagonist dies (forks RIGHT to a red stub box, diagonal death line,
  X / square cap by style).
- A split that is not a crossing (the world forks but nobody travels) still
  draws its circle in-lane with both tines vertical.
- Numbering: splits by story order; death tines take the scene number + B
  (e.g. `78B — He Dies`). Worlds that pre-date the protagonist get the star
  convention (`*`) and their ancestry stays off-chart.

## Joins (crossings)

- The survivor enters an already-running world. Drawn: vertical down the
  SOURCE lane from the split, horizontal across, green dot on the target lane,
  thread continues below the dot. Never an upward or backward arrow.
- The target lane was already running: its line starts at the TOP of the chart
  (dashed in dash-style = pre-history), with a grey "ALREADY RUNNING" chip.
- A joined lane shows only the protagonist's occupancy. The world's own life
  (before arrival, off-screen while there, after departure) is ONE grey
  "elsewhen" beat + faded tail — never the world's full biography.
- Abandoned branches "RUN ON" (blue chip, faded tail). The world doesn't end
  because the protagonist left it.

## The thread

- The green line is the film's path — exactly one continuous stroke from the
  opening node to the ending. If you can't trace it with a finger, the chart
  is wrong.
- Deaths end their lane; joins continue it. The thread never branches (that's
  what splits are for) and never reverses.

## Endings

- Choose where the story stops being drawn. Ending on the final split (the
  last death) leaves the final world a header and a join dot — present but
  unplayed. That restraint is expressive: the film may imply more than the
  chart states.
- An ending box (green) states the final image with its scene cite.

## Lettering

- Chart text reads as hand-lettered storyboard annotation: title case, not
  ALL CAPS. Lane acronyms stay as written.
- Legend at the foot, under "The Index / How to Read" — two or three lines
  that teach the grammar, because the audience for these charts has never
  seen one before.

## Citation and uncertainty discipline

- Every beat cites the script (`#scene`). Where a film has no scene numbers
  (studied from the print), cite the runtime instead — `46:32` — and say so
  in the legend. Never guess.
- Compress uncountable repeats into a labelled row/segment (`11+`). End on
  `?` when the final state is unknowable (`uncertain: true` on the ending —
  dashed "?" box, not the confident green). Uncertainty gets its own row,
  never a guess.
